TechNav

Step-by-step indoor directions inside Northwestern’s Tech building — find any room without getting lost in the hallways.

Open the app GitHub repository
Course prototype, not a maintained product.

TechNav was built as a Northwestern computer science course project. It is a working prototype: it may have bugs, its maps and photos may be incomplete, and it may stop being maintained or taken offline after the quarter ends. Do not rely on it as your only way to find a room.

What it is

TechNav gives turn-by-turn walking directions between rooms in the Tech building, from the basement through the fourth floor. It includes:

  • Room search with step-by-step instructions and a live route map.
  • Hallway photos at fixed points along your route, where volunteers have contributed them.
  • Built-in reporting: flag a wrong route, correct a room entrance, or contribute a hallway photo. No account needed.
  • A separate admin workspace where authorized reviewers check submissions before anything goes public.

Why you might want it

The Tech building is famously confusing — split levels, long repetitive hallways, and room numbers that don’t always sit where you’d expect. If you have a class, meeting, or office hour in Tech and don’t want to wander, TechNav tells you exactly where to turn. It runs in your phone browser, is free, and asks for nothing to get started.

How to get it

  1. On your phone, open technav-db7a8.web.app. There is nothing to install.
  2. Search for the room you’re going to and pick a starting point.
  3. Follow the steps. If something looks wrong, use the report button to tell us.

The source code is public at github.com/dguddanti/technav.

What information is collected

No account, no name, no email. The app signs you in with a random anonymous ID so your reports can be attributed without identifying you.

  • Reports you submit: the text you write, the floor and map pin you place, and route context (start, destination, step number) when you report from navigation.
  • Photos you submit: resized to at most 1600 px, re-encoded as JPEG, and stripped of their original metadata (including any location tags) before upload.
  • Admin sign-in only: administrators log in with Google, so Google provides their verified email address to confirm they are on the access list. Regular users never see a login screen.
  • Usage analytics: the production site uses Firebase Analytics to count aggregate usage. Development and test sessions are excluded.

The app never asks for your location. Every position in the app is a pin you tap on the floor plan — there is no GPS or live tracking.

Who sees it besides the developers

  • Authorized admins can read pending reports and their private photo evidence in order to review them. Admins are a short list of named team members; access can only be granted by an existing admin.
  • Everyone using the app can see content once an admin approves it: published hallway photos appear in navigation, and published entrances update search and routes.
  • Google/Firebase hosts the database, file storage, authentication, and analytics infrastructure, so submitted data passes through their services under their terms.

Nothing is sold or shared with any other third party. Pending (unapproved) reports and photos stay private to developers and admins.
